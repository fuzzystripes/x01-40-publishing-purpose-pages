Not an oracle.
An instrument.

Today's models are not intelligent. They have no goals, no plans, no agenda, and no idea what they are for. Here is why that is not a failure — and what these machines are actually for.

AI's purpose is not to be intelligent. It is to make intelligence less of a bottleneck.

01  ·  The premise

You're right. Mostly right.

An LLM is not a mind with a small database attached. It is a transformation engine over language: show it a situation, get back a plausible next move. That is the whole of it — and the absences matter more than the capabilities.

Input

A situation, described in words

Prompt, code, a document, a transcript. Everything arrives as text and leaves as text.

Engine

A distribution over what comes next

Pure pattern completion over learned statistical structure. No model of the world, only a model of how words follow words.

Output

An artifact that reads like someone meant it

Coherent, fluent, on-topic — and perfectly capable of being wrong with total confidence.

no goals of its own no persistent self no reliable world model no way to check its own work no memory between sessions no sense of what it doesn't know

One step of generation, spread across candidates. This — not a mind — is what produces every sentence these systems write.

02  ·  The correction

Not a store. A transformer.

“Only automated knowledge” undersells the thing. Retrieval was solved by search decades ago. What is actually new is the ability to compress, translate, restructure, recombine — and to produce artifacts that did not exist before.

The old toolWhat these machines add
Search→Finds what already exists. These systems rebuild the answer — compressing, restructuring and recombining it for the situation in front of you.
The archive→Stores the past. These systems make a new first draft from it, in the register, length and format you need.
The translator→Moves words between two languages. These systems move ideas between registers — technical to plain, past to present, one field to another.
The chart→Shows what was measured. These systems extract structure from text nobody had organised.
The blank page→Waits. These systems start the work — which was always the expensive part.
A book isn't intelligent either.
The instrument

A book stores someone else's cognition

Reliable, externalised, recombinable. It carries no goals of its own — and it is not less useful for that.

The point

Usefulness is not the same as intelligence

Every tool worth having lacks a mind. The mind is the user, not the instrument.

03  ·  The reframe

The bottleneck was never intelligence.

Ask why a job takes a week and the honest answer is rarely “nobody here was smart enough.” It is attention, bandwidth, memory, and the cost of the first draft. Those are the walls these machines take down.

Cost of making things
Value of deciding which things
What AI compresses
Surface the options Draft the artifact Search & summarise Draft code & analysis Translate & reformat Explore counterfactuals

Hours or days of grinding → seconds of plausible output. This side of the work was never intellectual; it was patient.

What stays scarce
  • Choose the objective
  • Weigh the trade-offs
  • Notice when it's wrong
  • Answer for the outcome

Not automated. Not delegable — because somebody is accountable for the answer.

So the human expert is not made unnecessary by a machine that cannot reason. They are made able to cover three times the cases, get a first draft in seconds, and spend their hours on the small fraction that genuinely requires judgement. Human intelligence stays the scarce input; the machine multiplies its throughput.

04  ·  The stack

The intelligence lives in the stack, not the weights.

A model on its own is a generator with no memory and no objectives. Wrap it in the right scaffolding and you get behaviour that looks agentic — and works, even if the model inside it understands nothing.

Inside the model

Next-token predictionThe only thing it actually does
Learned statistical structureEverything it knows, compressed as weights
Nothing elseNo direction, no continuity, no error correction

Around the model

A goalSupplied by a person or an organisation
Memory & contextFiles, notes, state — continuity across steps
Tools & retrievalSearch, code execution, APIs — hands and eyes
Verification & feedbackTests, checks, review — where errors get caught

The bare model generates; the stack acts. And the two ingredients that make the difference — selection and verification — are still supplied by humans and by cheap external checks. That is not a flaw. It is where the judgement comes from.

05  ·  Purpose

Four honest purposes.

Strip away the demos and the inevitability, and four uses survive scrutiny. None of them requires the machine to be intelligent — all of them require it to be useful.

01

Scale cognition

More thorough analysis, wider coverage, faster iteration — from the same headcount. One expert's attention, applied further.

Due diligence · code review · research breadth
02

Automate routine intellectual labour

Translation, formatting, triage, drafting, summarising — the work that never needed intelligence, only patience and volume.

Inbox · meeting notes · data cleanup · first-pass support
03

Serve as an instrument for thought

Simulate a viewpoint, argue the other side, generate counterfactuals, map a space you would not have reached. Cheap, fast, tireless.

Red teams · devil's advocate · scenario planning
04

Move scarcity from production to judgement

When generating becomes cheap, deciding becomes valuable. The work shifts from “what do we say” to “which of these is right, and do we stand behind it”.

Strategy · prioritisation · standards & quality
06  ·  The rule

If it is a plausibility machine, here is the rule that follows.

Delegate to the machine where a plausible answer is nearly as good as a correct one — or where correctness is cheap to check. Keep humans owning goals, trade-offs and accountability.

HIGH Cost of being wrong LOW
check: cheap  ·  wrong: cheap

Delegate freely

Wrong answers are cheap and mistakes are obvious. Hand it over and move on.

Formatting · transcription · boilerplate · summarising a document you already hold
check: cheap  ·  wrong: expensive

Generate many, verify hard

The answer may be badly wrong, but a machine can check itself cheaply. Verification is the work.

Code with tests · maths with a solver · claims with a citation you click
check: expensive  ·  wrong: cheap

Let it draft, humans refine

Checking is expensive and errors are survivable. Use it for breadth, then judge the output yourself.

Outlines · option sets · alternatives · tone and structure
check: expensive  ·  wrong: expensive

Human owns it end to end

Wrong is expensive and checking is expensive. This is where a machine is a drafting aid at most — and accountability cannot be delegated.

Medical decisions · legal judgement · hiring · anything irreversible
LOW Cost of checking the output HIGH
Read it as a map: the more expensive a mistake is, the higher the box; the harder the output is to check, the further right.

Notice that "is the AI smart?" is the wrong question to ask of a system like this. The right questions are how expensive is it to check this, and how expensive is it if I am wrong? Those two numbers decide how much of the work to hand over.

Tools have no purposes. They get them.

AI is not going to become intelligent, and it does not need to. It is the most powerful instrument yet built for extending a single mind — a way of multiplying the throughput of the intelligence that remains scarce.

And there is a second, quieter purpose, which is the one that should worry us if we get it wrong: these systems are instruments for being convinced. They are fluent, tireless, and unnervingly confident in the way that persuades. The questions still need someone accountable for the answer.

AI does not supply the judgement.
It removes everything that hides the lack of it.
